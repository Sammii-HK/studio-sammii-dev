# Short video scripts (5)

Drafts only. **Nothing recorded, rendered or scheduled.** These are 30 to 45 second talking-head scripts for you to record in PostReady/Studio, then post to TikTok, Reels and Shorts. The best fit is your personal build-in-public lane (Solopreneur), which already has a long video queue, so slot these in rather than adding volume. Each one ends with "link in bio" rather than saying a URL.

Format: HOOK (first 2 seconds, on screen as text too), BODY, CLOSE. About 120 spoken words each.

---

## 1. "Apple rejected my app because of one number"

**HOOK:** Apple rejected my app because of the number one point zero.

**BODY:** My app Strata used to be paid. When I made it free, I wanted everyone who'd already paid to keep everything. So the app checks which version you first downloaded. Sounds simple. Except in Apple's test environment, that value is "1.0" for every single account. So every App Store reviewer looked like an original buyer. Everything unlocked. No purchase screen. Rejected, because they couldn't find the in-app purchase. The fix was tiny: only count real build numbers, and ignore anyone who first bought after the switch to free.

**CLOSE:** I've written up every rejection like this one, with how to fix it. Link in bio.

## 2. "The button word that got my app rejected"

**HOOK:** One word on one button got my app rejected.

**BODY:** Before an app asks for your camera, a lot of apps show a little screen explaining why. Mine did. And the button said "Allow". Apple rejected it. Their reasoning: a button that says "Allow" looks like the real system permission prompt, so it's nudging people into saying yes. The fix was changing it to "Continue". Same screen, same flow, then the real prompt appears. And if someone says no, the app still has to work.

**CLOSE:** If you're building an app, I made a free checklist of the rejections that catch people out. Link in bio.

## 3. "Five rejections for the same app"

**HOOK:** Apple rejected my app five times for the same reason.

**BODY:** It's an astrology app. And Apple has a rule about crowded categories. They literally name fortune telling, next to dating and flashlight apps. I tried everything. Put journaling first. Changed the category. Appealed to the review board. Same answer every time, word for word. What I learned is that this isn't a bug you fix. It's a verdict on what your app is. Rearranging it doesn't change that. Only changing what the app is actually about does.

**CLOSE:** I write about building and shipping apps solo. Follow if that's your thing.

## 4. "My paywall was empty, and only Apple could see it"

**HOOK:** My paywall was empty, and the only person who could see it was the App Store reviewer.

**BODY:** Crystal Index has a premium subscription. On my machine the purchase screen looked fine. Well, it looked the same as it always does in the simulator, which can't load real products anyway. On the reviewer's iPad it said "Subscriptions are not configured yet". I'd never passed the product list into the paywall. Then I fixed that, and got rejected again, because the Upgrade button opened a teaser screen with no prices before the real one. Now it's one tap to a real price.

**CLOSE:** Test your purchases on a real device. And if you're stuck in review, link in bio.

## 5. "What a design engineer actually does"

**HOOK:** I'm a design engineer. Here's what that means in one minute.

**BODY:** Designers say "this should feel lighter." Engineers ask "which colour token?" My job is answering both in the same piece of work. I take a design, build it properly in the real codebase, and make the components reusable so the next screen is faster. I've done it for my own products: four iOS apps, a component library, a web app with paying subscribers. And I'm doing it for teams now, on contract, in London or remote.

**CLOSE:** If your team needs one, my case studies and rates are linked in my bio.
